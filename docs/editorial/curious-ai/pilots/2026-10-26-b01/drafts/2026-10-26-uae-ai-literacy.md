# Teaching children about AI can start without giving them a chatbot

*Examples from Dubai separate understanding the technology from using a public AI service independently.*

A teacher pretending to be a robot can introduce children to AI without anyone opening ChatGPT.

In [Ananya Bhattacharya's October 13 reporting for Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2026/uae-mandates-k12-ai-education-schools/), a Dubai primary-school leader describes activities in which children give a teacher increasingly precise instructions. Another example asks pupils to spot something an AI system has got wrong. The school describes teacher-led learning for younger children, rather than independent use of public generative chatbots.

The policy behind the broader discussion is older. [A May 4, 2025 announcement carried by the Emirates News Agency](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bji6z2n-ministry-education-introduces-curriculum-public) set out AI education from kindergarten to Grade 12 in UAE public schools, beginning in the 2025–26 academic year. It described age-specific activities, including stories and play for kindergarten.

These are different kinds of evidence. The ministry announcement states public policy. Interviews with named Dubai schools illustrate particular practices; they do not show what every UAE classroom does. Neither source demonstrates improved learning outcomes.

**Pulse interpretation:** debates about AI in schools become clearer when they separate learning about a system from handing a child an account. Recognizing that instructions matter, that outputs can be wrong and that a person must judge an answer are possible educational goals. Whether an activity achieves them needs evaluation.

The robot game offers a concrete starting point for that distinction. AI literacy can concern the questions children learn to ask, before it concerns which service they are allowed to use.
