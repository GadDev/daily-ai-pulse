# A tiny AI trained on racehorse names supplies this October's drawing prompts

*Janelle Shane's Botober experiment gives a small language model a narrow job—and leaves the drawing to people.*

A drawing prompt called “Expected Shoes” does not need a bigger AI model. It needs someone willing to decide what the shoes expected.

For this year's [Botober, announced September 30](https://www.aiweirdness.com/botober-2026/), Janelle Shane describes training a small character-based neural network on 42,000 registered racehorse names. Instead of drawing pictures, it supplies phrases for people to interpret during October.

Shane says the model runs locally on her laptop and was trained only on the data she supplied. That is her account of this particular setup; Pulse has not reproduced the training. The published prompt list is the artifact readers can inspect.

The creative division of labor is the interesting bit. A model produces an odd phrase. A person decides whether it suggests a horse, a drawing, a poem or something else. There is no requirement for the software to understand the eventual artwork.

**Pulse interpretation:** this is a useful reminder that an AI's awkwardness can be part of a deliberately chosen game. In a factual answer, a bizarre combination may be a defect. In an art prompt, it can be the invitation. The human still chooses the purpose and what is worth keeping.

This is an ongoing October activity, not a new model release or evidence that small models are universally more creative. Sometimes a strange little phrase is enough to get a person started.
