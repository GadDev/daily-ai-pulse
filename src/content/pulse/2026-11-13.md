Today's edition is about where AI systems acquire authority, and how to measure what follows. Google makes fast-versus-deep model routing a product decision; DeepMind narrows genetic variant prediction from tissue averages toward cell states; and Google's agent monitor publishes useful detection evidence alongside a particularly revealing weakness around poisoned memory. A2UI and AWS give developers shorter paths to agent interfaces and live web results, but neither removes the need for independent permission and input boundaries. Microsoft supplies named deployment baselines instead of blanket productivity promises. Finally, a peer-reviewed social-contagion study suggests that artificial participants may change adoption dynamics even without changing human preferences. Every claimed result remains scoped to the cited evidence; availability, simulation, first-party measurement and real-world outcomes are not interchangeable.
