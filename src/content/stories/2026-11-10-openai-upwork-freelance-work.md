The lazy version of the AI-and-jobs debate asks whether a tool replaces a worker. Real markets are messier.

New research from OpenAI and Upwork examines how requests for freelance work changed while ChatGPT use expanded. The partners report that some familiar services lost marketplace share, while AI-related projects combined more capabilities, involved more hours and carried larger budgets.

That does not settle the employment debate. It does provide something better than another CEO prediction: observable changes in project demand.

## What the study found

The analysis compares categories of ChatGPT activity with categories in Upwork postings. OpenAI says 43 of 63 analyzed work categories followed a pattern in which activities relatively common in ChatGPT lost Upwork share while less common activities gained share. The remaining 20 moved the other way.

The study also reports that clients increasingly combine tasks inside AI-related projects. A posting may require data work, software integration, domain judgment and content production rather than one narrowly defined service. AI-related projects had larger budgets and more work than other postings in the analyzed sample.

The observation windows matter. ChatGPT shares cover January 2025 through June 2026. Upwork comparisons use the first half of 2025 and the first half of 2026. This is a snapshot of two platforms during rapid adoption, not a timeless map of work.

## What the evidence does not prove

Correlation is doing a lot of work here.

People may use ChatGPT for writing because it is easy to try alone, while continuing to hire specialists for video, animation or work requiring production assets. Upwork clients and freelancers are not representative of every employer or occupation. Categories can gain or lose share because other categories change, even when absolute demand rises.

The platforms also shape behavior. ChatGPT makes some tasks cheap to attempt. Upwork exposes projects clients are willing to outsource. Comparing them reveals different choices, but not a controlled experiment in substitution.

OpenAI acknowledges that current ChatGPT activity does not capture the full capability frontier. The company calls that gap the capability overhang. That framing is plausible, but it is also convenient for a model provider: future capability remains a prediction until behavior and demand actually change.

## The project boundary is moving

The most useful signal is that AI-related work appears to bundle skills rather than reduce every job to prompting.

When routine production becomes cheaper, clients can expand scope. A small automation request can grow into data cleaning, integration, evaluation and maintenance. A content task can require research, asset production and distribution. The worker is not necessarily doing the same unit of work faster; the unit itself changes.

That has practical consequences for freelancers and teams. Narrow execution skills may be easier to automate or commoditize. Problem framing, verification, system integration and domain accountability become more valuable because they connect the pieces and absorb risk.

This does not mean every worker wins. Larger projects can concentrate spend among fewer suppliers. Clients may expect broader skill sets without paying proportionally more. Platform rankings and access to AI tools can distribute opportunity unevenly.

## What teams should measure

Organizations should avoid turning this report into a generic productivity claim. Track the work your own system changes:

- Which tasks disappear, shrink or move in-house?
- Which projects become possible only because AI lowers their starting cost?
- Does scope expand faster than delivery capacity?
- How much verification and rework is added?
- Are budgets shifting toward integration and accountability?
- Who captures the savings: clients, platforms or workers?

The answer will vary by function and market. A task-level ledger is more honest than a headline number about jobs.

## Pulse take

The study's best contribution is refusing the cartoon in which one prompt equals one vanished contract.

AI changes the shape of demand. Some work is absorbed by tools. Some becomes cheaper. Some projects become larger and stranger because clients can now afford to attempt them.

The uncomfortable question is not only how many jobs remain. It is whether workers can move into the broader, more accountable roles that the new project bundles require—and whether the market pays them for carrying that responsibility.
