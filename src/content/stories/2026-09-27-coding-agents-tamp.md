Researchers let coding agents interact with simulated task-and-motion planning environments, develop programs within a fixed budget, then freeze those programs and test them on unseen instances.

The authors report strong generalization relative to hand-engineered planners on the evaluated environments.

## Why it matters

Coding agents may be useful not only for application software, but as **program-synthesis systems that distill interactive exploration into reusable control logic**.
