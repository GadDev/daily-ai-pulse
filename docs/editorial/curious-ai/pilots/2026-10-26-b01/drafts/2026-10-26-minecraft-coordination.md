# Four AI players beat Minecraft; the tool access matters as much as the dragon

*An October 15 preprint reports a successful team run. Its limitations explain what kind of achievement it was.*

Four AI-controlled Minecraft players defeated the Ender Dragon after 27 hours and 14 minutes, according to a preprint by Maxence Faldor, Satinder Singh and Roberta Raileanu. The reported run included 605 in-game deaths. Persistence was evidently part of the job.

The [October 15 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22319v1) describes Gemini 4 Argon agents acting through Mineflayer, software that connects programs to Minecraft. They wrote code, received text describing the results and shared information. They were not looking at a screen and pressing keys as a human player would.

That distinction belongs beside the win. The paper says the interface exposed detailed game state, including information a person could not ordinarily see. The game also ran on Easy difficulty. This was one author-reported run, not a measured success rate or an independently reproduced result.

**Pulse interpretation:** the interesting comparison is between completing a goal and completing it under particular constraints. If an assistant gets a map that a person cannot see, the map is part of the explanation. It does not make the goal meaningless; it changes what the result demonstrates.

The paper was previously a source gap in Pulse's research queue. Its accessible primary account now supports this bounded explanation, not a claim that a new experiment happened today. A dragon has been defeated in a reported setup. How reliably the approach works—and what carries into other tasks—remains a separate question.
